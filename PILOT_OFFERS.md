# Pilot offers

I structure client work around a specific decision rather than an open-ended technology build. Each engagement begins with a data-readiness and risk check, defines the evidence needed for a useful decision, and ends with a documented recommendation and next step.

The durations below are typical planning ranges. Final scope depends on data availability, access requirements, stakeholder availability, and the level of operational integration required.

## Customer Conversation Intelligence

**Best for:** Customer-experience, product, support, and operations teams with large volumes of tickets, chats, survey comments, or other customer feedback.

**Decision supported:** Which recurring themes, likely drivers, and emerging risks deserve investigation or action—and whether an assisted review workflow is accurate and useful enough to continue.

**Typical duration:** 3–5 weeks.

### Inputs

- A representative, appropriately de-identified feedback sample
- The current taxonomy, routing rules, or reporting categories, if they exist
- The current review or triage process and its owners
- Subject-matter reviewers for taxonomy and evaluation decisions
- Applicable privacy, retention, and model-provider constraints

### Delivery approach

1. Define the triage decision, allowed uses, taxonomy, and escalation boundaries.
2. Assess the input sample and establish the current review baseline.
3. Build or configure an evidence-checked classification and analysis workflow.
4. Evaluate results with human reviewers, including category-level errors and ambiguous cases.
5. Deliver the dashboard, executive readout, operating recommendations, and next-stage decision.

### Deliverables

- Agreed taxonomy and decision contract
- Reproducible analysis or classification workflow
- Human-evaluation results and disagreement review
- Reviewable dashboard or reporting output
- Escalation, correction, and human-review guidance
- Executive pilot readout and recommended next step

### Acceptance criteria

The scope will define numerical thresholds after the data-readiness review. At minimum, the pilot should demonstrate:

- complete reconciliation between supplied records and reported outputs;
- traceable evidence for every output intended to inform review;
- documented quality by important category, not only an overall accuracy score;
- visible correction and escalation paths;
- comparison with the current process on reviewer effort or decision usefulness; and
- a clear go, iterate, or stop recommendation.

### Not included unless separately scoped

- Unreviewed automated customer decisions or routing
- Processing sensitive or regulated text without approved controls
- Production authentication, enterprise integration, or long-term monitoring
- Guaranteed cost reduction, retention improvement, or model-quality claims before measurement

## AI Launch and Chatbot Measurement

**Best for:** Teams launching—or already operating—a chatbot, agent-assist tool, automated support workflow, or other customer-facing AI capability.

**Decision supported:** Should the initiative launch, expand, iterate, pause, or roll back based on customer, operational, and quality evidence?

**Typical duration:** 2–4 weeks, with optional ongoing monitoring.

### Inputs

- Product objectives, launch dates, rollout cohorts, and known changes
- Conversation, event, resolution, transfer, repeat-contact, and escalation data
- Customer-satisfaction or quality-review data where available
- Existing KPI definitions and current-state reporting
- Operational policies, failure categories, and risk guardrails

### Delivery approach

1. Define the decision, baseline, primary measures, guardrails, and failure conditions.
2. Validate event coverage, metric definitions, comparison groups, and observation windows.
3. Analyze adoption, containment, resolution, repeat contact, satisfaction, quality, and risk signals.
4. Separate supported findings from limitations and alternative explanations.
5. Deliver a go, iterate, pause, or rollback readout and monitoring plan.

### Deliverables

- KPI and measurement contract
- Baseline and data-quality assessment
- Launch-impact or experiment analysis
- Segment, failure-mode, and guardrail findings
- Decision-ready stakeholder readout
- Monitoring ownership, thresholds, and review cadence

### Acceptance criteria

- Metrics reconcile to agreed source events and denominators.
- The analysis uses an appropriate comparison design and documents causal limits.
- Primary measures and guardrails are reported together.
- Important segments and failure modes are visible without unsupported claims.
- The final readout names the recommended action, owner, monitoring period, and rollback condition.

### Not included unless separately scoped

- Rebuilding the chatbot or production application
- Claiming causal impact where the available design cannot support it
- Continuous monitoring after the agreed delivery period
- Legal, regulatory, security, or accessibility certification

## Fractional AI Product and Delivery Leadership

**Best for:** Teams with AI work that is stuck between discovery, pilot, evaluation, and production ownership.

**Decision supported:** What should the team build next, what evidence is required, who owns each decision, and what must be true before launch or scale?

**Typical format:** Ongoing part-time engagement. The first two weeks normally establish the current-state assessment, priorities, decision owners, and working cadence.

### Inputs

- Current roadmap, backlog, architecture, vendor, and model decisions
- Existing prototypes, evaluation results, risks, and unresolved assumptions
- Stakeholder interviews across product, engineering, data, operations, and governance
- Delivery constraints, target dates, and available team capacity

### Deliverables

- Prioritized roadmap and decision-oriented backlog
- Pilot and evaluation plan
- Delivery cadence, ownership model, and decision log
- Launch-readiness and governance gates
- Stakeholder updates, risk escalation, and dependency management

### Acceptance criteria

- Every active initiative has an owner, intended decision, evidence requirement, and next milestone.
- The backlog distinguishes discovery, validation, build, launch, and monitoring work.
- Launch gates and material risks are explicit.
- Stakeholders have an agreed decision and reporting cadence.
- Blocked or low-value work is surfaced for a stop, defer, or rescope decision.

### Not included unless separately scoped

- Acting as legal, compliance, privacy, or security approver
- Replacing a full-time engineering or organizational leader
- Hands-on software development beyond the agreed delivery scope
- Guaranteeing production approval or adoption

## Analytics and Experimentation Support

**Best for:** Product, growth, operations, and customer teams that need a defensible answer to a high-value question.

**Decision supported:** Which action should be taken based on KPI movement, an experiment, a launch, a target population, or another measurable business change?

**Typical duration:** 2–3 weeks for a focused analysis or experiment-design sprint.

### Inputs

- The business decision, available options, audience, and deadline
- Relevant data extracts and metric definitions
- Experiment assignment or intervention timing where applicable
- Known operational changes, constraints, and guardrails
- Stakeholders who can validate assumptions and act on the recommendation

### Deliverables

- Decision and metric contract
- Data-quality and analytical validation
- Reproducible analysis or experiment design
- Effect, uncertainty, segment, and business-impact assessment
- Executive decision memo or presentation
- Follow-up measurement and monitoring plan

### Acceptance criteria

- The analytical population, timing, metrics, and exclusions are documented.
- Results reconcile to source counts and agreed definitions.
- Effect size, uncertainty, practical significance, and limitations are reported together.
- Exploratory findings are distinguished from confirmatory evidence.
- The final output recommends an action and states what evidence should be collected next.

### Not included unless separately scoped

- Production dashboard engineering or long-term data-platform work
- Causal claims unsupported by the available design
- Data collection that does not yet exist
- Ongoing reporting after the agreed handoff

## How an engagement starts

1. **Initial conversation:** Identify the decision, urgency, available data, and stakeholders.
2. **Readiness check:** Review a schema, sample, metric definition, or current workflow without requesting unnecessary sensitive data.
3. **Written pilot scope:** Confirm the decision, inputs, responsibilities, timeline, deliverables, acceptance criteria, exclusions, and stop conditions.
4. **Kickoff:** Begin only after access, privacy, ownership, and review expectations are agreed.
